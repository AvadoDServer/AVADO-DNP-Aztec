Install an Ethereum node first

Aztec is built on Ethereum. Your Aztec node reads Ethereum through the Ethereum node and the beacon node on this AVADO, so it does not have to trust anyone else's servers.

Let Aztec read Ethereum transaction details

Aztec needs to look inside some Ethereum transactions (a “trace”). Your Ethereum node does not offer that yet.

One click changes a setting of Geth on this AVADO so it offers traces. Geth then restarts, which takes about a minute: if you stake ETH on this box, your validators may miss one or two attestations (a few cents).

Technically: “debug” is added to Geth's list of RPC services. Other apps on your AVADO can then use these functions too.

Nethermind on this AVADO offers traces out of the box. If you use another Ethereum node, turn on its “debug” or “trace” functions.

Become an Aztec sequencer

Sequencers take turns to build Aztec blocks and to vote on other sequencers' blocks. In return they earn AZTEC and fees.

  • You need 200,000 AZTEC to stake one sequencer. You stake from your own wallet on the Aztec staking website. Your stake, and the right to take it back, stay with that wallet. The box never sees it.
  • The box keeps the sequencer's own keys: an Ethereum key that signs and pays the small Ethereum fees (send it about 0.01 ETH), and a second key for voting.
  • If the box is off for long, the sequencer misses its turns and can lose part of the stake as a penalty. Keep the box online.
Not 200,000 AZTEC? About delegation

People with less AZTEC can delegate it to a staking provider instead of running a sequencer. Becoming a staking provider yourself (running sequencers with other people's stake) is possible too, but it is an advanced setup: see Aztec's staking provider guide.

Set it up

  1. Make the sequencer keys with the button below. The box shows 24 recovery words once: write them on paper.
  2. Send about 0.01 ETH to the sequencer address shown after step 1, for the Ethereum fees.
  3. Stake. Download the staking file, open stake.aztec.network, connect the wallet that holds your AZTEC, choose “Stake”, then “Run your own Sequencer”, upload the staking file and confirm in your wallet.
  4. Wait. New sequencers join a queue. This page shows “Active” when yours has its turn.

Remove the sequencer keys

The node stops being a sequencer. If you are staked, you then miss your turns and can be penalised: first take your stake out on stake.aztec.network. You can make the same keys again later from your 24 words. Type REMOVE to confirm.

Settings

For the best connection to other sequencers, let port 40400 (TCP and UDP) through your router to this AVADO. It also works without, with fewer connections.
